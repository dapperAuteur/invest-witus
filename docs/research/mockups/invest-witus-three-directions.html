Invest.WitUS: three design directions

One method, one account, three ways to design the app around it. Every direction shows the same thing: the owner running 12 minus 1 momentum (rank by the last 12 months, skipping the most recent month; hold the top 10; review monthly) on an Alpaca paper account, with each rule linking to the lesson that teaches it.

Mockups, not the product. All numbers and symbols are illustrative sample data, not prices or recommendations. Research behind each direction: 01-ux-benchmarks-and-three-directions.md in this folder.

A · The Plan and the Logbook (calm)

A written plan with a logbook beside it. One thing to do next. Every step off the plan becomes a dated, explained exception. Reminders follow the calendar, never price moves.

Borrows: Betterment's on track / off track with fixes; M1's target allocation; Actual's rules from what you do; Edgewonk's rule-following rate; Vanguard's measure of success as staying on track.

Risk: may be too quiet to bring people back; cramped for methods with many rules.

B · Method Console (dense)

The owner supervises a running system. Status at the top, checks with evidence, a queue of orders the rules produced, paper results against the backtest's expected range. Live trading unlocks only through a promotion checklist, on separate keys.

Borrows: Ghostfolio's X-ray rules; QuantConnect's backtest to paper to live; Public's order queue; TradingView alerts; Alpaca's separate paper and live hosts; USWDS and GOV.UK tables.

Risk: dense live numbers invite tinkering (the FCA found engagement features raise trading); heaviest for beginners and for accessibility.

C · The Lesson Lab (course-first)

A textbook with a lab bench. The lesson is the home screen; each lesson ends with the one rule it gives you, and the lab applies that rule to your paper account. Rules switch on as you reach them. Breaking a rule reopens the chapter that taught it.

Borrows: QuantConnect's Learning Center; Backtrader's step-by-step quickstart; Composer's readable rule blocks; Fidelity's learning pages with no trade buttons; Snowball's per-strategy numbers.

Risk: needs course content for every method; slow for experienced users; chapter progress can drift into gamification.

4.2 Twelve months, skip one

The method ranks each stock by its return over the past year, but leaves out the most recent month. Researchers who studied momentum found that the last month tends to reverse, so including it blurs the signal. In this lesson you see that in your own list.

[Lesson text continues, with its sources: the academic papers and Gray and Vogel, Quantitative Momentum, cited, never copied.]

The rule this lesson gives you

Rank by the return from 12 months ago to 1 month ago. Ignore the last month.
