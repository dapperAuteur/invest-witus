# Invest.WitUS FAQ: what to ask about any investing app

Invest.WitUS is a learning tool: you pick an investing method taught in a course, practice it on a paper (simulated) account, and the app holds you to that method's rules. This page answers the questions a careful person asks about any investing app, names the bodies that would govern a professional version of it, and lists the laws behind those answers, with links to the primary text.

## Report a bad actor

> **If someone took your money, pressured you, or will not give it back, report it now. Reporting is free, you do not need a lawyer, and these desks act on reports from ordinary people.** This app does not take complaints about other companies; the bodies below do.
>
> - **A broker, adviser, or anyone selling an investment:** the SEC's tips, complaints and referrals form (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-d, n.d.-e): https://www.sec.gov/submit-tip-or-complaint
> - **A brokerage firm or its people (lost money, an unauthorized trade, a broken promise):** FINRA's complaint form (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-c): https://www.finra.org/investors/need-help/file-a-complaint
> - **Anyone selling or advising in Indiana:** the Indiana Securities Division's portal (Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division, n.d.-a, n.d.-b): https://securities.sos.in.gov/general-information/file-a-complaint/
> - **Another state:** find your state's securities regulator through NASAA (North American Securities Administrators Association, n.d.-b): https://www.nasaa.org/contact-your-regulator/
> - **A bank account, loan, card, or payment app:** the CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, n.d.-b): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/
> - **Futures, commodities, or crypto derivatives:** the CFTC's Division of Enforcement takes reports of "violations or any other suspicious activities" (Commodity Futures Trading Commission, n.d.-b): https://www.cftc.gov/complaint
> - **A scam, a deceptive sales pitch, or an unfair business practice:** the FTC's home page points "Report fraud" to ReportFraud.ftc.gov (Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-a): https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/
> - **Any fraud that happened online (a fake app, a romance or "guaranteed returns" scheme, a hijacked account):** the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, the "central hub for reporting cyber-enabled crime" (Federal Bureau of Investigation, n.d.): https://www.ic3.gov/
>
> Keep the names, dates, screenshots and statements. If you are not sure which desk, file with the SEC or your state regulator; they forward what is not theirs.

## A. Questions to ask about any investing app, and our answers

### Is this app a registered investment adviser or broker?

Why it matters: registered advisers and brokers owe duties to clients and can be looked up and complained about. An unregistered tool owes you none of that.

Our answer: no. Invest.WitUS is registered with no one and never recommends a security; it only checks whether your actions follow the plan you set. Federal law defines an investment adviser as a person who, "for compensation, engages in the business of advising others" about "the advisability of investing in, purchasing, or selling securities" (Investment Advisers Act of 1940, 2024). The operator treats the app as education only, charges nothing, and will ask a securities lawyer before admitting other users.

### Who holds my money?

Why it matters: an app that holds money can lose it, freeze it, or vanish with it.

Our answer: the app never holds money or securities; a brokerage does. In phase 1 that is an Alpaca paper account; Alpaca says its Paper Trading API "does not require real money" (Alpaca, n.d.-d). A live account, if you ever connect one, stays at the broker; the app only reads from it or passes your orders to it.

### Is my money insured, and by whom?

Why it matters: "insured" means different things at a bank and at a brokerage, and neither covers a bad investment.

Our answer: SIPC covers customers of a failed member brokerage: "The limit of SIPC protection is $500,000, which includes a $250,000 limit for cash," and "SIPC does not protect against the decline in value of your securities" (Securities Investor Protection Corporation, n.d.). FDIC insurance covers bank deposits "to at least $250,000 at each FDIC-insured bank," not stocks, bonds, or funds (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, n.d.). A paper account holds nothing real, so neither applies to it. Invest.WitUS insures nothing.

### Can I lose money, and can I lose more than I put in?

Why it matters: margin and short selling can leave you owing more than you deposited.

Our answer: with real money, yes, you can lose what you invest. Losing more than that takes borrowing or shorting; the SEC says "Borrowing money to trade in stocks is always a risky business" (Securities and Exchange Commission, 2005). The method taught alongside this app buys with cash only, and the plan's rules refuse an order that would break that.

### Is paper trading realistic?

Why it matters: a simulation misleads if you expect its results to repeat with real money.

Our answer: not fully, and the vendor says so. Alpaca writes that "paper trading is only a simulation," that it "does NOT simulate dividends," and that it ignores slippage, market impact, queue position, and regulatory fees (Alpaca, n.d.-f). It also carries no emotion. Treat paper results as practice at following rules, not as a forecast.

### What fees do I pay, and what does the broker charge?

Why it matters: fees are the one cost you can read in advance.

Our answer: Invest.WitUS charges nothing in phase 1. The broker's fee schedule governs; Alpaca's says it "does not charge commissions, except as described below" and lists pass-through regulatory fees on sells (Alpaca, n.d.-a). Read it before any live account. A paper account pays no real fees.

### Who is behind this app, and how do I reach them?

Why it matters: an unreachable operator cannot fix an error or honor a deletion request.

Our answer: an individual operator in Indiana who builds the WitUS family of learning tools. Use the Contact link in the footer of every page. No one else has access in phase 1.

### What does the app do with my data, and can I delete it?

Why it matters: a trading app sees your holdings, your orders, and your broker keys.

Our answer: in phase 1 the app holds the operator's own paper data and the waitlist: a name and an email address, nothing more. Broker keys stay on the server, paper keys are the default, no data is sold, and no one's holdings are shown to anyone else. To leave the waitlist or delete an account, use the Contact link. Plans and journal export to CSV.

### What happens if the app shuts down?

Why it matters: apps end; your money and records should not end with them.

Our answer: your brokerage account lives at the broker, untouched. Your plans and journal export to CSV at any time, so keep a copy. The waitlist is deleted. Nothing you own is held here.

### What does "information, not advice" mean in practice?

Why it matters: "advice" has a legal meaning; "information" does not.

Our answer: the courses explain methods and what the evidence says about them. The app shows your plan's rules and whether your actions follow them. Neither tells you what to buy or sell, when, or how much, and neither looks at your income, goals, or taxes. If you want someone to choose for you, that is advice, and you want a registered adviser.

### What if the app's numbers are wrong?

Why it matters: software has bugs, and a wrong balance can cause a wrong decision.

Our answer: the broker's statement is the record. The app's practice budget is its own number for your plan, not a broker balance, and every signed-in page says whether you are on paper or live. If a figure looks wrong, trust the broker, stop, and report it through the Contact link.

### Does anyone check the courses?

Why it matters: anyone can publish a course; qualified review separates a lesson from a rumor.

Our answer: Learn.WitUS has reviewer sign-off. An invited, qualified reviewer reads a lesson as it stands and records one verdict: cleared, cleared with a correction, or not cleared. The verdict is tied to the exact lesson text, so any edit resets it until a reviewer reads it again. The lesson page shows its review state.

### How do I check whether a broker or adviser is legitimate?

Why it matters: the lookup takes minutes and is the best protection against fraud.

Our answer: FINRA's BrokerCheck for brokers and firms (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-b); the SEC's Investment Adviser Public Disclosure for advisers (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-a); and your state regulator, found through NASAA (North American Securities Administrators Association, n.d.-b), which in Indiana is the Securities Division (Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division, n.d.-d). Invest.WitUS appears in none of them.

### What is the day-trading rule now?

Why it matters: brokers used to require $25,000 of equity from "pattern day traders"; that changed in 2026, and some brokers are still in transition.

Our answer: on April 14, 2026, the SEC approved FINRA's amendment to Rule 4210 to "eliminate provisions relating to 'pattern day traders'" and "the minimum equity requirement of $25,000 for pattern day traders" in favor of intraday margin standards (Securities and Exchange Commission, 2026). FINRA's notice says "The effective date of the amendments is June 4, 2026," and firms "will be permitted to phase in their implementation over a period of 18 months, until October 20, 2027" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, 2026). FINRA's investor page now says "you must maintain a minimum of $2,000 in equity in your margin account" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-g), and Alpaca's docs confirm the $25,000 minimum "has been removed" (Alpaca, n.d.-e). Check your own broker's rule; none of this touches a cash-only paper account.

### What are taxes on gains, and what is a wash sale?

Why it matters: taxes are a real cost of trading that a paper account never shows.

Our answer: the IRS says a gain or loss is long-term "if you hold the asset for more than one year," and that net losses can offset up to $3,000 of other income a year (Internal Revenue Service, n.d.-b). A wash sale is when "you sell or trade stock or securities at a loss and within 30 days before or after the sale you" buy substantially identical securities, including in an IRA; the loss is disallowed and added to the cost of the new shares (Internal Revenue Service, 2026; Internal Revenue Code, 2024). A method that sells and rebuys the same names inside that window can trip this. Paper trades have no tax effect.

### Who do I complain to?

Why it matters: the right desk acts faster.

Our answer: about the app, the Contact link. About a broker or adviser: FINRA, which says to "complain in writing" if you lost money or saw an unauthorized trade (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-c); the SEC's "Submit a Tip or Complaint" page (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-d, n.d.-e); and the Indiana Securities Division, by portal or paper form (Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division, n.d.-a, n.d.-b). About a bank account, loan, or payment product: the CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, n.d.-b). Every link is in "Report a bad actor" at the top of this page.

### Who should not use this app?

Our answer: anyone who wants someone else to pick their investments. Anyone under 18 (see COPPA below). Anyone trading money they cannot afford to lose. Anyone who wants price alerts or performance charts; the app shows neither.

## B. Who would govern this if it were a professional tool

Invest.WitUS is registered with none of these bodies. Each would come into play if the app, or its operator, crossed into the activity it oversees.

**U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).** The federal securities regulator, whose mission is "protecting investors, maintaining fair, orderly, and efficient markets" (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-b). It would govern this app if the operator became a federally registered adviser. https://www.sec.gov/about/mission

**FINRA.** "A private not-for-profit membership organization" and "a self-regulatory organization for member broker-dealers" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-a). It would govern this app if the operator became a broker-dealer. https://www.finra.org/about

**Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division.** Registers broker-dealers and investment advisers in Indiana and takes complaints; "Investment advisers conducting business or with a place of business in Indiana must register" (Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division, n.d.-c). As the operator's home state, it is the first registrar the app would meet. https://securities.sos.in.gov/general-information/investment-adviser/

**NASAA.** "A voluntary association whose membership consists of 67 state, provincial, and territorial securities administrators" (North American Securities Administrators Association, n.d.-a). Not a regulator; it is how you find yours. https://www.nasaa.org/about-us/

**SIPC.** "A non-profit corporation created by Congress" that protects brokerage customers when a member firm fails (Securities Investor Protection Corporation, n.d.). It covers the brokerage behind a live account, never the app. https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects

**FDIC.** "The FDIC only insures your money if it is in a deposit account at an FDIC-insured bank" (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, n.d.). This app never touches a bank account. https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/

**Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB).** "Established by Congress to regulate the offering and provision of consumer financial products and services" (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, n.d.-a). It would apply if the app offered a loan or payment service; it does not. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/the-bureau/

**Federal Trade Commission (FTC).** Its mission is "to vigorously enforce the law to protect Americans from anticompetitive, unfair, and deceptive business practices" (Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-e). It reaches every business's advertising and data practices, this app included. https://www.ftc.gov/about-ftc/mission

**Internal Revenue Service (IRS).** "The IRS mission is to provide America's taxpayers top quality service" and to enforce the tax law (Internal Revenue Service, n.d.-a). It applies to you on real gains and losses, never on paper. https://www.irs.gov/about-irs

**CFTC and NFA.** The CFTC's mission is to "promote the integrity, resilience, and vibrancy of the U.S. derivatives markets through sound regulation" (Commodity Futures Trading Commission, n.d.-a); "NFA is the industrywide, self-regulatory organization for the U.S. derivatives industry" (National Futures Association, n.d.). They would apply only if the app touched futures, options on futures, or crypto derivatives; it does not. https://www.cftc.gov/About/AboutTheCommission and https://www.nfa.futures.org/about/index.html

**Looking a firm or person up.** BrokerCheck: https://brokercheck.finra.org/. Investment Adviser Public Disclosure: https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/. Your state regulator, through NASAA: https://www.nasaa.org/contact-your-regulator/.

**Filing a complaint.** SEC: https://www.sec.gov/submit-tip-or-complaint (tips form: https://www.sec.gov/submit-tip-or-complaint/tcr-disclaimer). FINRA: https://www.finra.org/investors/need-help/file-a-complaint. Indiana Securities Division: https://securities.sos.in.gov/general-information/file-a-complaint/ or the portal at https://lcm.securities.sos.in.gov/file-a-complaint. CFPB: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/.

## C. Laws and rules that would govern the app

Each entry says what the law covers, why you should care, and where the text is. Whether a law reaches this app is a lawyer's question; the operator treats the app as education only and will ask a securities lawyer before admitting other users.

**Investment Advisers Act of 1940.** Defines an "investment adviser" (quoted in section A) and excludes a teacher whose advice is "solely incidental" to teaching and "the publisher of any bona fide newspaper, news magazine or business or financial publication of general and regular circulation" (section 202(a)(11); Investment Advisers Act of 1940, 2024). In *Lowe v. SEC* (1985) the Supreme Court held that public newsletters fell within the publisher exclusion because they "do not offer individualized advice attuned to any specific portfolio." Why you should care: this is the line between a tool and an adviser. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap2D-subchapII-sec80b-2.htm and https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181

**Securities Act of 1933.** Requires registration of securities offered for public sale; the SEC describes its aims as giving investors "financial and other significant information concerning securities being offered for public sale" and barring fraud (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-c; Securities Act of 1933, 2024). Why you should care: it is why public companies publish prospectuses and financial statements. The app offers no securities. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap2A-subchapI-sec77e.htm

**Securities Exchange Act of 1934.** "With this Act, Congress created the Securities and Exchange Commission" and gave it "the power to register, regulate, and oversee brokerage firms, transfer agents, and clearing agencies" (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-c); section 15 requires brokers and dealers to register (Securities Exchange Act of 1934, 2024). Why you should care: your broker is registered under this Act, which is what puts it in BrokerCheck. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap2B-sec78o.htm

**Investment Company Act of 1940.** Regulates companies "engaged primarily, or propose to engage primarily, in the business of investing, reinvesting, or trading in securities" (Investment Company Act of 1940, 2024), including mutual funds (Securities and Exchange Commission, n.d.-c). Why you should care: the funds a method might use are investment companies. The app pools no one's money and is not one. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap2D-subchapI-sec80a-3.htm

**Indiana Uniform Securities Act (Indiana Code 23-19).** Indiana's own securities law, administered by the Securities Division, whose page says advisers in Indiana "must register" unless exempt or excluded under IC 23-19-1-2(15), with a representative who passed the Series 65 or 66 (Indiana Secretary of State, Securities Division, n.d.-c). Why you should care: for a small adviser, state registration comes long before any federal threshold. Statute text: link to come (the General Assembly's site served only a script shell on the check date). Regulator's page: https://securities.sos.in.gov/general-information/investment-adviser/

**FINRA rules a retail customer meets.** Rule 2111 (Suitability): a member "must have a reasonable basis to believe that a recommended transaction or investment strategy" is "suitable for the customer" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-d). Rule 2210 (Communications with the Public): communications "must be fair and balanced" and "may not predict or project performance" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-e). Rule 4210 (Margin Requirements), as amended in 2026, now defines an "intraday margin level" and an "intraday margin deficit" (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, n.d.-f, 2026). Why you should care: these bind your broker, not this app, and they are the standard to hold any app's claims to. https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2111, https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2210, https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210, and https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10

**Regulation Best Interest.** A broker-dealer, "when making a recommendation of any securities transaction or investment strategy involving securities" to a retail customer, "shall act in the best interest of the retail customer at the time the recommendation is made" (Regulation Best Interest, 2026). Why you should care: it is what you are owed when a broker recommends something; this app never does. https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-17.xml?part=240&section=240.15l-1

**Regulation T.** Issued by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System; "Its principal purpose is to regulate extensions of credit by brokers and dealers" (Regulation T, 2026). Why you should care: it is the rule behind margin, the way to lose more than you deposited. The taught method uses cash only. https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-12.xml?part=220&section=220.1

**Regulation S-P and the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act Safeguards Rule.** The Act states "the policy of the Congress that each financial institution has an affirmative and continuing obligation to respect the privacy of its customers and to protect the security and confidentiality" of their data (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, 2024). Regulation S-P applies that duty to brokers, dealers, investment companies, and SEC-registered advisers through privacy notices and written safeguarding policies (Regulation S-P, 2026a, 2026b). The FTC's Safeguards Rule covers other financial institutions, "any institution the business of which is engaging in an activity that is financial in nature" (Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information, 2026; Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-d, 2024). Why you should care: these rules make a financial firm guard your data; whether a free education tool is one is a question for the lawyer, and the app guards broker keys as if it were. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap94-subchapI-sec6801.htm, https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-17.xml?part=248&section=248.30, and https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-16.xml?part=314&section=314.2

**Indiana Consumer Data Protection Act (Indiana Code 24-15).** Indiana's privacy law. The Attorney General's guide says "Starting January 1, 2026, we will enforce Indiana's new Consumer Data Protection Act (CDPA)," and that it reaches businesses that "Control or process the personal data of 100,000 or more Indiana residents" in a year, or at least 25,000 residents while earning more than half of gross revenue from selling personal data (Indiana Attorney General, n.d.). Why you should care: this app is far below those thresholds, so the deletion and export promises above are the operator's own, not the Act's. Statute text: link to come (same script-shell problem). Attorney General's guide: https://www.in.gov/attorneygeneral/files/Indiana-Consumer-Data-Protection_Consumer-Bill-of-Rights_CDPCBOR.pdf

**SEC Marketing Rule.** "It is unlawful for any investment adviser registered or required to be registered under section 203 of the Act" to "disseminate any advertisement that violates any of paragraphs (a) through (d)" of the rule, which restrict testimonials and performance claims (Investment Adviser Marketing, 2026). Why you should care: it is why a registered adviser's ads carry disclosures. This page carries no testimonial and no performance claim. https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-17.xml?part=275&section=275.206(4)-1

**FTC Act section 5 and the FTC Endorsement Guides.** "Unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce, are hereby declared unlawful" (Federal Trade Commission Act, 2024). The Guides say "Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser," and a material connection "must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously" (Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 2026; Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-c). Why you should care: this covers every business's claims, this app included. Any future testimonial from a person given early access will say so. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap2-subchapI-sec45.htm and https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-16.xml?part=255

**CAN-SPAM.** The federal law for commercial email: senders "should not mislead recipients as to the source or content of such mail," and recipients "have a right to decline" further mail (CAN-SPAM Act of 2003, 2024). The FTC's guide: honest headers, prompt opt-out handling, a postal address, and an opt-out route (Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-a). Why you should care: the waitlist is an email list, and every message must carry an unsubscribe route and a postal address. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap103-sec7701.htm and https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

**COPPA.** Defines a "child" as "an individual under the age of 13" (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, 2024); the FTC's rule requires an operator that collects a child's data to "Obtain verifiable parental consent" first (Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule, 2026; Federal Trade Commission, n.d.-b). Why you should care: this is one reason the app is for adults only; the operator has not researched minors' use with schools. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap91-sec6501.htm and https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-23/title-16.xml?part=312&section=312.3

**Tax rules: capital gains and wash sales.** Under 26 U.S.C. § 1091, a loss is disallowed when "substantially identical stock or securities" are acquired within 30 days before or after the sale (Internal Revenue Code, 2024). Publication 550 and Topic 409 explain holding periods, rates, and the yearly loss limit (Internal Revenue Service, n.d.-b, 2026). Why you should care: a method that sells and rebuys the same names can lose the deduction; the app's journal records the dates. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapO-partVII-sec1091.htm, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p550, and https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409

**Vendor terms as contracts.** The vendors' current terms are contracts you accept; they govern over anything here and they change, so read them before you connect anything. Alpaca's Customer Agreement says "I understand that My Account is self-directed" and "Alpaca provides no investment, legal or tax advice" (Alpaca, n.d.-b); its Terms and Conditions say Alpaca Securities "does not make recommendations of any kind" (Alpaca, n.d.-c). SnapTrade is not used in version 1. If it ever is: its Developer Terms say "Client may use the Free Plan for testing and personal use only" (SnapTrade, 2026), its End User Terms say "SnapTrade does not provide investment advisory services or brokerage services in any manner or form" (SnapTrade, 2025), and its compliance policy requires that signals be "pull-based" and automated orders "managed by a rules-based and non-stochastic approach" (SnapTrade, 2024). Alpaca: https://alpaca.markets/disclosures, https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/AcctAppMarginAndCustAgmt.pdf, https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/TermsAndConditions.pdf, and https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/BrokFeeSched.pdf. SnapTrade: https://snaptrade.com/developer-terms-of-use, https://snaptrade.com/terms-and-conditions, and https://snaptrade.com/compliance-policy
