How to read a 10-K

A 10-K is the company's annual report to the SEC, a 10-Q its report for each of the first three quarters, and an 8-K a short report of a major event (results, a merger, a new chief executive). The 10-K follows a set order. Four parts answer most questions:

  • Item 1, Business: what the company sells, where, and to whom.
  • Item 1A, Risk Factors: what the company says could hurt it, most important first.
  • Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis: management explains the year's results in its own words.
  • Item 8, Financial Statements: the audited income statement, balance sheet and cash flows, with notes. The Key numbers below come from these.

Read a 10-Q the same way for a quarter; its statements are not audited. A figure means more beside the same figure a year earlier than on its own.

The SEC's guide: How to Read a 10-K (opens in a new tab). Information, not advice.
